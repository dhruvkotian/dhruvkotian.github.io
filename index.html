I am an undergraduate Mechatronics Engineering student. My work focuses on embedded systems, edge artificial intelligence and intelligent connected devices.

I develop and experiment with ESP32-based systems involving neural-network deployment, quantised inference, computer vision, robotics, industrial IoT and sensor-based condition monitoring. Most of what I build starts at the sensor and ends at a decision — firmware that samples, filters, runs a small model, and acts without waiting for a network round-trip.

I care about systems that survive the field, not just the bench: watchdogs, failsafes, persistent state, bounded tool calls and telemetry you can actually trust. Lately that has meant distilling rule engines into sub-kilobyte int8 networks, speaking Modbus to industrial meters, and giving microcontrollers a safe way to call a local language model.

That curiosity also pushes into vacuum hardware — building a low-pressure chamber from scratch to watch plasma form, and learning that seals, feedthroughs and electrode geometry matter as much as the high voltage. Outside the firmware I prototype the whole stack, from wiring and power budget to the dashboard that makes the data legible.

Seven builds recovered from the bench: wearables, industrial telemetry, predictive maintenance, local AI and a hand-built plasma chamber. Every one of them ran on hardware I could hold.

[01] NASA SPACE APPS 2026

HyperWatch

A wearable astronaut health monitor running a 13→16→8→6 int8 risk model on an ESP32-S3. Five NASA-mapped risk domains, a cardiac-event flag, mission context, OLED pages and NVS persistence — with the same model compiled for the web dashboard.

  • ESP32-S3
  • int8 MLP
  • PPG
  • NVS
  • WASM

[02] LOCAL AGENT

ParoClaw

An ESP32-S3 local AI agent. It connects to an Ollama model over the LAN and exposes a bounded set of firmware-side tools — GPIO, ADC, PWM and NeoPixel — with persistent LittleFS memory and deterministic fallbacks when the model returns nothing useful.

  • Ollama
  • Tool calling
  • C++
  • LittleFS
Repository
[03] INDUSTRIAL

EM2M ESP32 Modbus

An Arduino driver for the Selec EM2M energy meter over Modbus RTU and RS485. HAL-style API, bulk register reads, retries and cached updates — built for industrial panels where a dropped frame should not drop the reading.

  • Modbus RTU
  • RS485
  • Industrial IoT
  • Arduino
Repository
[04] TINYML

AI Tunnel Fan

TinyML condition monitoring for tunnel booster fans. Temperature sensing plus on-device anomaly classification, so a failing fan can be flagged without streaming raw data to a server.

  • TinyML
  • TFLite Micro
  • Predictive maintenance
Repository
[05] EDGE AI

AI EV BMS

An experimental ESP32 battery-management node that combines sensor data with lightweight edge AI for battery condition analysis — a small model watching voltage, current and temperature instead of a threshold table.

  • BMS
  • Edge AI
  • Sensors
  • ESP32

[06] HV / VACUUM

Vacuum Plasma Chamber

A low-pressure plasma chamber built from scratch to study vacuum systems, high-voltage electronics and plasma formation. Designed and assembled the chamber, tested fittings and seals, built custom feedthroughs, and integrated a pump and gauge before experimenting with discharge in air and under vacuum.

  • How plasma behaves under reduced pressure
  • How electrode geometry affects discharge
  • Vacuum sealing and leak testing
  • Practical limits of HV systems and custom hardware
  • Vacuum
  • HV
  • Plasma
  • Feedthroughs
