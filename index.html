About − 240 m

I am an undergraduate Mechatronics Engineering student. My work focuses on embedded systems, edge artificial intelligence and intelligent connected devices.

I develop and experiment with ESP32-based systems involving neural-network deployment, quantised inference, computer vision, robotics, industrial IoT and sensor-based condition monitoring. Most of what I build starts at the sensor and ends at a decision: firmware that samples, filters, runs a small model, and acts without waiting for a network round-trip.

I care about systems that survive the field, not just the bench: watchdogs, failsafes, persistent state, bounded tool calls and telemetry you can actually trust. Lately that has meant distilling rule engines into sub-kilobyte int8 networks, speaking Modbus to industrial meters, and giving microcontrollers a safe way to call a local language model.

That curiosity also pushes into vacuum hardware: building a low-pressure chamber from scratch to watch plasma form, and learning that seals, feedthroughs and electrode geometry matter as much as the high voltage. Outside the firmware I prototype the whole stack, from wiring and power budget to the dashboard that makes the data legible.

[03] MONITORING

DC Fan Vibration Monitor

An ESP32-S3 and MPU6050 rig that samples a cooling fan at up to 1 kHz, computes per-axis RMS and an FFT on the board, and serves a local dashboard with the waveform, spectrum and a baseline anomaly check. The healthy reference is captured at boot and kept in RAM.

  • Up to 1 kHz MPU6050 capture over I2C
  • Per-axis RMS and total vector RMS
  • Hann-windowed FFT and dominant peak on-device
  • Boot baseline, local dashboard and JSON API

  • Web dashboard

[04] INT8 / SIMD

ESP32-S3 INT8 Transformer

A 260K-parameter decoder running entirely on an ESP32-S3, with the KV cache stored as int8 in PSRAM and attention mapped onto the 128-bit PIE integer SIMD unit. At 512 context it reaches 166.6 decode forwards/s (168.25 sampled tokens/s), 2.898× the sampled-rate fp32 baseline.

  • int8 KV cache with an fp32 SRAM hot window
  • 128-bit PIE attention kernels and dual-core head split
  • 512-token context, batch size one, fully offline
  • Raw logs, negative results and the exact build config
  • ESP32-S3
  • int8
  • KV cache
  • SIMD
  • PSRAM
